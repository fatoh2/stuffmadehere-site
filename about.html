The shop

Eight motion-capture cameras aimed his bow at flying targets. Here they watch the shop: move across the floor and every head turns to follow you; leave it alone and they tour the tools.

  • Tormach 24Rthe CNC mill; most of the metal parts come off it
  • Plasma cuttercuts the sheet; its particle filter was video two
  • 3D printersthe first video bent steel with tools printed on them
  • OptiTrack cameraseight of them, the ones that aimed the bow
  • Blank cartridgeswhat the bat, the axe and the nut cracker ran on
  • 17,342 lbwhat it all weighed, by his count, in 2021

The heads are on motors with a speed and an acceleration limit, so they lag and settle like real pan heads. When all eight agree, the point is locked and the nearest tool lights up.

What he builds

How he works

  1. Every build is the same fight: a problem nobody has solved, a machine that does not work yet, and the weeks until it does. The failures stay in the video at full length, which is why the successes land.
  2. His wife tests most of the machines and is not easily impressed. The second hoop video went up under the name “Automatic hoop unless you’re my wife”: she is the one who has to beat them.
  3. He builds the tool that builds the thing: a pumpkin carver, a chainsaw robot, a plotter that forges handwriting, a pick-and-place for an all-white jigsaw. The machine is the point; the pumpkin is the excuse.

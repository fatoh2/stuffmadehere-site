Won’t let it fall

A table that will not let the ball roll off. Two servos tilt the plate; a controller reads where the ball is and how fast it is going and leans the table against it.

Flick the ball with your finger or mouse. The hard part is the lag: the servos cannot turn instantly, so the controller has to tilt before the ball gets there, not after. His hoop solves the same problem with a prediction instead of a feedback loop.

Sign small, engrave big

A pantograph. Four bars in a parallelogram, one pivot bolted down, and whatever you trace on the small pad the pen cuts three times larger on the plate.

Draw on the pad. The geometry is two hundred years old and still the cheapest way to scale a hand; the machine signs the channel’s name first, then it is yours.

A trebuchet that learns

It fires, sees where the shot lands, moves its own counterweight and fires again, until the ball drops into the bucket.

Drag the bucket and it starts over. The release scatters a little, as a real sling does, so one correction is never enough. Every build misses first; this one keeps the log.
